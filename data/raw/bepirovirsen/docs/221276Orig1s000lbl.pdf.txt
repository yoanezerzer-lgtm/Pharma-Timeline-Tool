There have been isolated cases of ALT increases associated with an increase in total bilirubin up to 4 x ULN that resolved when treatment was permanently discontinued. 5.2 Thrombocytopenia HIBSAGO may cause reductions in platelet count at any time during treatment that may result in sudden and unpredictable thrombocytopenia that can be life-threatening. In the HIBSAGO clinical trials, two participants reported serious adverse reactions of thrombocytope nia <50 × 10⁹/L, including one participant with immune thrombocytopenia [see Adverse Reactions (6.1)] . In addition, a gradual, dose-dependent decline in platelet count was observed in most patients that continued to worsen while on HIBSAGO. Platelet Monitoring and Dosing Platelet count must be measured prior to initiation of treatment. Do not initiate HIBSAGO in patients with a platelet count below 100 x 10 9 /L [see Contraindications (4)] . In patients with platelet count below the normal range but ≥100 x 10 9 /L, rule out cirrhosis before initiating HIBSAGO [see Contraindications (4)] . During and after HIBSAGO treatment, follow recommended monitoring and treatment recommendations for platelet count as specified in Table 2 [see Dosage and Administration (2.4)] . In all patients, the minimum recommended on-treatment monitoring of platelet counts is weekly through Week 24, but more frequent monitoring may be necessary. In addition, HIBSAGO treatment interruption or permanent discontinuation may be necessary based on the results of platelet testing. If platelet count falls below 50 x 10 9 /L, permanently discontinue treatment with HIBSAGO and consider corticosteroid treatment in consultation with an expert. In all patients with abnormal values at the time of HIBSAGO discontinuation, obtain repeat platelet counts 4 and 12 weeks post-treatment to assess for platelet count recovery, as appropriate. Symptoms of Thrombocytopenia Symptoms of thrombocytopenia can include unusual or prolonged bleeding (e.g., petechiae, easy bruising, hematoma, subconjunctival bleeding, gingival bleeding, epistaxis, hemoptysis, irregular or heavier than normal menstrual bleeding, hematemesis, hematuria, hematochezia, melena), neck stiffness, or atypical severe headache. Instruct patients to report to their healthcare provider immediately if they experience any symptoms of thrombocytopenia. If a patient develops signs or symptoms of thrombocytopenia, treatment with HIBSAGO must be paused and a platelet count obtained as soon as possible [see Dosage and Administration (2.4)] . Additional Considerations Consider the risks and benefits of treatment with HIBSAGO when treating patients who may be particularly susceptible to platelet count decreases, e.g., elderly patients, or patients with bleeding diathesis/coagulopathy, history of major bleeding events or patients taking antiplatelets, anticoagulants, or drugs that lower platelet count [see Drug Interactions (7.1)] . 5.3 Worsening Renal Function In the HIBSAGO clinical trials, mean eGFR declined within 4 weeks of initiation of HIBSAGO and continued to decline during treatment. These changes were generally reversible after treatment discontinuation. The mechanism of the decrease in eGFR is not clear [see Adverse Reactions (6.1)] . Obtain eGFR and uACR in all patients prior to initiation of treatment. The clinical trials excluded participants with moderate or severe renal impairment (eGFR ≤60 mL/min/1.73m 2 ) or uACR >0.3 mg/mg; insufficient data are available on the safety of HIBSAGO in these populations [see Use in Specific Populations (8.6)] . Consider the risks and benefits of HIBSAGO when treating patients that may be more susceptible to inflammatory and/or immune effects, e.g., solid organ transplant recipients, patients with autoimmune hepatitis, or patients with a history of vasculitis or conditions associated with vasculitis (including systemic lupus erythematosus, cryoglobulinemia, or rheumatoid arthritis). Bepirovirsen is designed to bind a conserved 20-nucleotide site (nucleotides 1581 5’-GCACTTCGCTTCACCTCTGC-3' 1600) present in HBV mRNAs (including the pregenomic RNA [pgRNA], precore, preS1, preS2/S, and X mRNAs), forming a DNA:RNA heteroduplex that recruits endogenous ribonuclease H1 (RNase H1) to catalyze irreversible hydrolysis of the viral RNA strand at the 3’-O-P bonds, resulting in degradation of HBV mRNA and reduction in HBV DNA and viral proteins, including HBsAg. The 20-nucleotide binding site is present within transcripts derived from both cccDNA and integrated HBV DNA. Bepirovirsen reduced the level of HBV RNA transcripts in a dose-dependent manner in HepG2.2.15 cells (HBV genotype D) using transfection-based delivery resulting in EC 50 values of 70 nM (16 hours) and 122 nM (48 hours) as measured by two RT-qPCR primer/probe sets targeting pgRNA, preS1, and preS2/S mRNAs. 13 NONCLINICAL TOXICOLOGY 13.1 Carcinogenesis, Mutagenesis, Impairment of Fertility Carcinogenesis In a 2-year carcinogenicity study in mice, bepirovirsen sodium was administered by subcutaneous injection weekly at doses of 3, 10, and 30 mg/kg (approximately 0.05, 0.2, and 0.5 times the RHD, respectively, based on BSA comparison). In male mice, statistically significant increases in tumor incidences were observed compared to controls in liver (hepatocellular carcinomas at ≥10 mg/kg; hepatocellular adenomas, combined hepatocellular adenomas/carcinomas, hemangiosarcomas, and combined hemangiosarcomas/hemangiomas at 30 mg/kg), in stomach (adenomas and combined adenomas/adenocarcinomas at 30 mg/kg), and at the injection sites (fibrosarcomas at 30 mg/kg). Increased tumor findings have been observed in 2-year carcinogenicity studies in mice dosed subcutaneously with phosphorothioate ASO class drugs; the clinical relevance is unknown. MEDICATION GUIDE HIBSAGO (hib sah' goe) (bepirovirsen) injection, for subcutaneous use What is the most important information I should know about HIBSAGO? HIBSAGO may cause serious side effects, including: • flare-ups in liver enzyme levels during treatment. HIBSAGO may cause increases in liver function blood tests. Tell your healthcare provider right away if you get any of these symptoms: o upper right stomach pain o nausea or vomiting o dark or “tea-colored” urine o loss of appetite o yellowing of your skin or the white part of your eyes • low platelet counts (thrombocytopenia) . HIBSAGO may cause your blood platelet levels to drop at any time during your treatment. When your platelet count is too low, your body cannot form blood clots properly. Low platelet counts can happen suddenly and without warning and can be life-threatening. Stop using HIBSAGO and tell your healthcare provider right away if you get any of these symptoms: o unusual bruising or a rash of tiny reddish - purple spots o abnormal or heavy periods (menstrual bleeding) o bleeding from skin cuts that does not stop or oozes o sudden severe headaches or neck stiffness o bleeding from your gums or nose o bleeding into the whites of your eyes o blood in your urine or stools Your healthcare provider will do blood tests to check your liver and platelet count, before you start HIBSAGO, during treatment, and if you have abnormal blood tests for up to 24 weeks after you stop HIBSAGO. It is important that you make sure you get these blood tests done . What is HIBSAGO? HIBSAGO is a prescription medicine used with other medicines called nucleos(t)ide analog reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NrtIs), to treat adults with chronic (long-term) hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection without advanced liver disease (cirrhosis): • who have been on NrtI treatment for at least 6 months and have undetectable HBV deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), and • who have low levels of HBV surface antigen (HBsAg less than or equal to 3,000 IU/mL) Your healthcare provider will perform tests to make sure HIBSAGO is right for you. It is not known if HIBSAGO is safe and effective in children less than 18 years of age. It is not known if HIBSAGO is safe and effective in people who also have hepatitis C virus (HCV) or hepatitis D virus (HDV) infection. HIBSAGO is not recommended in people with HDV infection as it may increase the chance of flare-ups in liver enzymes. Who should not use HIBSAGO? Do not use HIBSAGO if you have: • a condition where your liver is currently not working as well as it should • have advanced liver disease (cirrhosis) • low platelet blood counts. Talk to your healthcare provider if you are not sure if you have any of these conditions. Before using HIBSAGO , tell your healthcare provider about all of your medical conditions, including if you: • have bleeding problems or a history of bleeding problems • have kidney problems • have received an organ transplant • have problems with your immune system (autoimmune disease), or have a history of blood vessel inflammation (vasculitis) • have hepatitis D • are pregnant or plan to become pregnant. It is not known if HIBSAGO will harm your unborn baby. • are breastfeeding or plan to breastfeed. HIBSAGO may pass into your breast milk. Talk to your healthcare provider about the best way to feed your baby during treatment with HIBSAGO. Tell your healthcare provider about all the medicines you take, including prescription and over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, and herbal supplements. Using HIBSAGO with certain other medicines may increase the risk of side effects. Especially tell your healthcare provider if you take: • medicines that prevent your blood from clotting or that lower your blood platelet count. Reference ID: 5889023
