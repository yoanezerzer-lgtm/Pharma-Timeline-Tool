Avoid concomitant use with certain proton pump inhibitors, and stagger concomitant use with H2-receptor antagonists and antacids [see Dosage and Administration (2.2), Drug Interactions (7.2), Clinical Pharmacology (12.3)]. 6 ADVERSE REACTIONS Because clinical trials are conducted under widely varying conditions, adverse reaction rates observed in the clinical trials of a drug cannot be directly compared to rates in the clinical trials of another drug and may not reflect the rates observed in practice. 6.1 Clinical Trials Experience The safety of HOVILPRI was evaluated in 206 adults treated with HOVILPRI and an additional 100 healthy adult participants exposed to HOVILPRI in clinical trials. Immunocompromised Adults with Mucocutaneous HSV Infection that is Refractory (with or without Documented Resistance) to Treatment with Acyclovir, Valacyclovir, or Famciclovir: The safety of HOVILPRI was evaluated in a Phase 3, randomized, open-label, comparator-controlled trial, PRIOH-1 (Part C), in immunocompromised participants with mucocutaneous (mucosal and/or cutaneous lesions) HSV infection that was refractory (with or without documented resistance) to treatment with acyclovir, valacyclovir, or famciclovir [see Clinical Studies (14)] . Participants received HOVILPRI (400 mg loading dose on Day 1 followed by 100 mg once daily) or Investigator’s Choice treatment (IV foscarnet, IV/topical cidofovir, or 5% topical imiquimod) for up to 28 days until all lesions healed, extended up to 42 days if lesions were improving. A total of 101 participants were randomized 1:1 and treated with either HOVILPRI or the Investigator’s Choice. Across the 51 participants treated with HOVILPRI, the mean duration of treatment was 27.1 days (range 6–43). The adverse reaction (all grades) occurring in more than 5% of participants receiving HOVILPRI was headache, which occurred in 6% and 4% of the HOVILPRI and Investigator’s choice participants, respectively. Adverse reactions were reported in 22% of HOVILPRI-treated participants, compared with 54% of participants in the Investigator’s Choice treatment arm. Discontinuations due to adverse drug reactions occurred in 2% and 20% of participants, respectively. Laboratory Abnormalities Hepatic laboratory abnormalities were observed in both treatment groups in PRIOH-1, Part C. ALT elevations ≥ 2.5 × upper limit of normal (ULN) were reported in 4/51 (8%) participants receiving HOVILPRI and 1/50 (2%) receiving Investigator’s Choice, while AST elevations ≥ 2.5 × ULN were reported in 3/51 (6%) and 2/50 (4%) participants, respectively. One participant discontinued HOVILPRI due to elevated liver enzymes. The safety of HOVILPRI was also evaluated in a non-randomized, open-label, multicenter, single-arm Phase 3 trial, PRIOH-1 (Parts D and F) in immunocompromised adults with acyclovir/foscarnet (ACV/FOS) refractory HSV infection or who were FOS intolerant (N=35). A fertility study of pritelivir in rats (50, 150 and 500 mg/kg/day) showed adverse effects on male fertility at 500 mg/kg/day. Male rats dosed at 500 mg/kg/day exhibited direct effects on epididymal sperm counts, sperm motility and morphology and a minor increase in severity in lumen detritus in the epididymis. At 150 mg/kg/day, male rats exhibited slight reduction of epididymal sperm counts as well as minor changes in sperm morphology. The effects were confirmed to be reversible after 6 weeks of discontinuing pritelivir with no lasting adverse effects on male reproduction. Male- mediated effects on early embryonic development occurred at 150 and 500 mg/kg/day (i.e., increase in fetal loss in treated and untreated females). No effects on fertility or the offspring occurred at 50 mg/kg/day approximately 6-times the human exposure (AUC) at the therapeutic dose of 100 mg. 8.4 Pediatric Use The safety and efficacy of HOVILPRI have not been established in pediatric patients 18 years of age and younger. 8.5 Geriatric Use Of the 51 immunocompromised participants treated with HOVILPRI in the Phase 3 PRIOH-1 Part C trial, 20% (10/51) were 65 years and older and 4% (2/51) were 75 years of age and older (range 19-84 years) [see Clinical Studies (14)] . In the event of an overdose, patients should be monitored for adverse reactions and managed with supportive care, as appropriate. Due to the high plasma protein binding of pritelivir , hemoperfusion or dialysis is unlikely to significantly reduce systemic exposure of pritelivir. In Vitro Drug Interaction Studies CYP enzymes: As pritelivir is primarily metabolized by non-enzymatic hydrolysis, leading to the formation of AIC090015 and AIC090105, clinically relevant drug-drug interactions with pritelivir as a CYP enzyme substrate are not expected. Pritelivir does not inhibit CYP1A2, CYP2A6, CYP2B6, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP2E1, or CYP3A4/5 at clinically relevant exposures. Pritelivir does not cause clinically relevant induction of CYP1A2, CYP2C9, or CYP2C19. Pritelivir does not cause time-dependent inhibition of any CYP enzymes. Transporters: Pritelivir is a substrate of both P-glycoprotein (P-gp) and BCRP efflux transporters in vitro , but is not a substrate of OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OATP2B1, organic cation transporter (OCT)1, or organic anion transporter (OAT)1. Because pritelivir has a high absolute oral bioavailability of 72%, co-administration of P- gp or BCRP inhibitors is not expected to impact the oral absorption of HOVILPRI to a clinically relevant extent. Metabolites: The major circulating metabolites of pritelivir, AIC090015 and AIC090105, are not expected to inhibit CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, or CYP3A4 to a clinically relevant extent. No clinically significant differences in the pharmacokinetics of OAT substrates are predicted when co- administered with HOVILPRI 100 mg once daily. 12.4 Microbiology Mechanism of Action Pritelivir is a HSV helicase-primase inhibitor. It is active against both HSV types, HSV-1 and HSV-2, and prevents de novo synthesis of virus deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) through inhibition of the viral helicase- primase complex. Kinetic analysis of the enzyme complex pUL5-pUL52-6xHis revealed the IC 50 value of 30 nM. Antiviral Activity Pritelivir exhibits an apparent 50% effective concentration (EC 50 value) between 0.01 μM and 0.07 μM in cell culture infected with HSV-1 or HSV-2 (laboratory strains and clinical isolates), depending on cell type, virus strain/isolate, and method used. The median EC 50 values of pritelivir were 26 nM (average 26 nM, range 10-45 nM, n=27) and 24 nM (average 30 nM, range 7-70 nM, n=32) against HSV-1 and HSV-2 clinical isolates, respectively. PATIENT INFORMATION HOVILPRI (hoe-vil-pree) (pritelivir) tablets, for oral use What is HOVILPRI? HOVILPRI is a prescription medicine used in adults with weakened immune systems (immunocompromised) to treat lesions caused by herpes simplex virus (HSV) infection that have not improved with the medicines acyclovir, valacyclovir, or famciclovir. HOVILPRI does not cure HSV infection. It is not known if HOVILPRI is safe and effective in children less than 18 years of age. Before taking HOVILPRI, tell your healthcare provider about all of your medical conditions, including if you: • are pregnant or plan to become pregnant. It is not known if HOVILPRI will harm your unborn baby. • are breastfeeding or plan to breastfeed. It is not known if HOVILPRI passes into your breast milk. Talk with your healthcare provider about the best way to feed your baby if you take HOVILPRI. Tell your healthcare provider about all the medicines you take , including prescription and over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, and herbal supplements. HOVILPRI may affect the way other medicines work, and other medicines may affect the way HOVILPRI works. Especially tell your healthcare provider if you take medicines used to lower stomach acid, including: • proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) (such as esomeprazole, rabeprazole, omeprazole, lansoprazole, or pantoprazole). • H2-receptor blockers (such as famotidine, ranitidine). • antacids (medicines such as Tums, Mylanta, Maalox). See “How should I take HOVILPRI?” and “What should I avoid while taking HOVILPRI?” if you take any of these medicines. Know the medicines you take. Keep a list of them to show your healthcare provider and pharmacist when you get a new medicine. How should I take HOVILPRI? • Take HOVILPRI exactly as your healthcare provider tells you to take it. • The usual dose of HOVILPRI is: o On Day 1 (first day of treatment): take 400 mg by mouth (four 100 mg tablets) at one time. o After Day 1: take 100 mg by mouth (one 100 mg tablet) 1 time a day. • Take HOVILPRI with or without food. • Take HOVILPRI as prescribed by your healthcare provider until your HSV lesions have healed. • If you take medicines that reduce stomach acid: o If you take an H2-receptor blocker (such as famotidine, ranitidine), take HOVILPRI at least 2 hours before or 12 hours after taking the H2-receptor blocker. o If you take an antacid (such as Tums, Mylanta, Maalox), take HOVILPRI 2 hours before or 2 hours after taking the antacid. • If you miss a dose of HOVILPRI, take it as soon as you remember. If you don’t remember until the next day’s scheduled dose, take 2 tablets at that time. • If you take too much HOVILPRI, call your healthcare provider, or the Poison Help line (1-800-222-1222), or go to the nearest hospital emergency room right away. What should I avoid while taking HOVILPRI? Avoid taking the following proton pump inhibitor medicines during treatment with HOVILPRI: • esomeprazole or rabeprazole • omeprazole or lansoprazole (daily dose above 20 mg) • pantoprazole (daily dose above 40 mg) Talk to your healthcare provider before taking any of these medicines. What are the possible side effects of HOVILPRI? The most common side effect of HOVILPRI is headache. Call your doctor for medical advice about side effects.
